# Is solar cheaper than nuclear in Europe?

## Short answer
The available sources do not provide Europe‑specific levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) estimates for either solar PV or nuclear power, so a direct conclusion about which technology is cheaper in Europe cannot be drawn from the evidence given.

## What the evidence shows
- Solar PV capital expenditure (CAPEX) in Europe varies by country and project size: utility‑scale (>1 MW ground‑mount) systems are reported in the range of roughly **€580‑€900 /kWp**, while residential (<30 kWp) installations can reach up to **€1 620 /kWp**【5】.  
- Solar PV capacity factors in Europe lie between **11 % and 15 %**, corresponding to annual yields of about **960‑1 300 MWh per MW** of installed capacity【19】.  
- Grid‑integration and storage costs needed to manage solar’s intermittency are estimated at **$8‑$50 per MWh**, whereas the system costs associated with nuclear’s baseload output are much lower, ranging from **$1‑$3 per MWh**【4】.  
- Policy support has driven solar’s LCOE down in some European markets: feed‑in tariffs and other incentives in Italy reduced solar PV LCOE from about **€250/MWh in 2010 to €50/MWh by 2020**【19】.  
- For nuclear, the sources note that mechanisms such as zero‑emission credits and carbon pricing (e.g., the UK carbon floor price) can improve its economics, but they do **not supply any European nuclear CAPEX, OPEX, LCOE, capacity factor, or decommissioning‑cost figures**【4】.

## Where sources disagree
There is no explicit disagreement among the provided excerpts; the limitation is a lack of comparable nuclear cost data rather than conflicting statements.

## Limitations
- No Europe‑specific nuclear CAPEX, OPEX, LCOE, capacity factor, or decommissioning cost estimates are given, preventing a lifetime‑cost comparison.  
- Solar OPEX values are not quoted in the material, and the integration‑cost range for solar is broad ($8‑$50/MWh) without a precise European‑wide figure.  
- The evidence on subsidies and carbon pricing is limited to country‑level examples (Italy, UK) and does not quantify their net effect across all European states.  
- Consequently, the sources cannot establish whether solar is cheaper than nuclear in Europe overall.
