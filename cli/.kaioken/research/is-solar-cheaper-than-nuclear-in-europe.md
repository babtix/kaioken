# Is solar cheaper than nuclear in Europe?

## Short answer
The available data show that utility‑scale solar PV in Europe has a typical levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) of about €87/MWh, with most observations between €43‑€168/MWh)【20】, but the sources do not provide a comparable LCOE figure for nuclear power in Europe. Consequently, we cannot definitively state whether solar is cheaper than nuclear based solely on the supplied evidence.

## What the evidence shows
- Solar PV LCOE in the EU27 (2018) ranged from €43 to €168/MWh, with three‑quarters of observations below €112/MWh and a typical value around €87/MWh【20】.  
- The same LCOE methodology includes capital, O&M, fuel (zero for solar), and decommissioning costs【16】.  
- The Wood Mackenzie report cited confirms that its LCOE dataset for Europe covers nuclear as well as utility‑scale solar, yet the excerpt does not give a specific nuclear LCOE value【15】.  
- No other cited source supplies a nuclear LCOE (including capital, O&M, fuel, and decommissioning) for Europe.

Thus, while solar’s cost range is quantified, the nuclear counterpart is missing from the evidence.

## Where sources disagree
There is no direct disagreement among the provided sources; they simply do not overlap on a nuclear LCOE figure to compare with the solar numbers.

## Limitations
The key limitation is the absence of a specific European nuclear LCOE (including all cost components) in the cited material. Without that figure, a direct cost‑competitiveness assessment between solar and nuclear cannot be made. Obtaining a comparable nuclear LCOE estimate (or a range) from a reliable European source would resolve this gap.
